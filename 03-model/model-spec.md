# Model Spec — Sidewalk Capital Investment Case

Spec first, cells second. The workbook is not started until this spec and the
assumptions log are stable. One workbook, six tabs, every input cell colored
and traced to an assumptions-log ID.

## Tabs

1. **INPUTS** — every parameter, one place. Each row: value, unit,
   assumptions-log ID (L1, B4, F1...), rating. No hardcoded numbers anywhere
   else in the workbook. Scenario switch cell (Base / Upside / Downside) and
   funding-level toggle live here.
2. **CLAIMS** — baseline expected annual liability from the 2015–2021 series
   (L1–L3), flat in real terms. Per-option reduction = rollout share ×
   mitigation effectiveness (E1, ESTIMATE — dominates tornado by design).
3. **OPTIONS** — three columns, one per option:
   (A) do nothing; (B) assessment + ramping/slicing at scale — cost = target
   rollout share × cost-per-location-mitigated (ESTIMATE, anchored to B5's
   $900K/4 rampers); (C) replacement acceleration via CIP — funding level
   toggle vs. the $16.1M/yr need (V1), city-responsibility share only (B3).
4. **NPV** — per-option cash-flow table over horizon F2: outlays vs. avoided
   claims, discounted at F1. Net benefit vs. option A, BCR, payback year.
   Liability applies to the full hazard population (L6); repair cost to the
   ~50% city share (B3).
5. **SENSITIVITY** — one-way tornado on: E1 mitigation effectiveness (the
   headline driver), cost-per-location-mitigated, claim frequency & severity,
   unit replacement cost, discount rate, B3 responsibility split, escalation
   rate. Two-way table on the two biggest drivers.
6. **OUTPUT** — the one-page view the memo screenshots: recommendation
   number, scenario range, tornado chart.

## Rules

- Every ESTIMATE-rated input MUST appear in the tornado. No exceptions.
- The memo may only cite figures that exist on the OUTPUT tab.
- B3 (responsibility split) is TD-sourced at ~50/50 but sensitivity-tested
  0–100% anyway — it is an estimate by the TD, not measured data.
- The workbook skeleton is generated by `build_model.py` (structure +
  formulas + traceability), then refined by hand in Excel. The script is the
  reproducible scaffold, not the finished model.
